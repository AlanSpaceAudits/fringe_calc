FRINGE CALCULATOR

N = 2·D·v² / (λ·c²) — Michelson & Morley 1887

Interactive demonstration of the classical fringe-shift formula derived in Michelson & Morley (1887), American Journal of Science 34: 333-345, p. 336.

The parallel arm round-trip time is 2D·c/(c²-v²); the perpendicular arm is 2D/√(c²-v²). The path-length difference is D·v²/c² per orientation. Rotating the apparatus 90° flips the sign of the difference, so the fringe displacement scales as 2D·v²/(λc²).

Drag the sliders, pick a scenario, or toggle Lorentz contraction to see the parallel arm shrink by √(1-β²) and null the predicted shift.

Cyan pulse — light traveling on the parallel arm (along the wind).

Magenta pulse — light on the perpendicular arm.

Source disc — laser, color matches λ.

Diagonal slab — beam splitter (50/50 plate).

Bottom strip — fringe pattern as seen by an observer; horizontal position of bright bands shifts with N mod 1.

Generalized form (Wang, Zheng, Yao, PRL 93, 143901 (2004)): Δφ = (4π/cλ) · ∮ v·dl. Linear segment with velocity v and length Δl at angle θ: Δφ = 4π·v·Δl·cos(θ) / (cλ), equivalently ΔN = 2·v·Δl·cos(θ) / (cλ). Rotating loop recovered via Stokes' theorem (∮v·dl = 2A·Ω): Δφ = 8π·A·Ω·cos(θ) / (cλ), ring-laser beat Δf = 4A·Ω·cos(θ) / (λL). The line-integral form is independent of the type of motion and the refractive index of the waveguide.
vlin column: v = ΔN · c · λ / (2 · Δleff), with Δleff = perimeter L for rotational rows and the reported segment v for linear rows.

vimplied (classical MMX): v = c · √(N·λ / 2D). From SME bound: v = c · √(2·Δc/c). vlin (Wang generalized Sagnac): v = N·c·λ / (2·Δleff) with Δleff = 2·D for MMX geometry (full round-trip optical path). Note that the Wang formula is first-order in v/c whereas the classical MMX prediction is second-order, so the two interpretations of the same observed N yield very different v values.
